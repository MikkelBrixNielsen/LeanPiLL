\section{Metatheory}\label{sec:metatheory}
This section describes the metatheoretic results for the typed, process, and environment transition systems defined in \cref{subsec:semantics}. The development is contained in the \path{SessionFidelity/} namespace and concerns the mechanised \pimll{} fragment.

\subsection{Session Fidelity}\label{subsec:session-fidelity}
Session fidelity states that well-typed processes evolve according to their session types and that their associated linear resources evolve consistently with each process transition. The mechanisation establishes the two projection results from typed transitions to process and environment transitions. The converse lifting direction is developed through typability subject reduction, with its remaining proof obligation isolated to the \texttt{res} case. Completing this case would finalise the typability-preservation result for the multiplicative fragment.

\paragraph{Erasure simulations} As shown by \citet[Theorem~4.7]{abs-2106-11818}, erasure induces a strong bisimulation between $\mlts{p}$ and $\mlts{d}$. The mechanisation currently establishes the projections from typed derivation steps to their process and environment behaviours. The files \path{ProcStep.lean} and \path{EnvStep.lean} contain the corresponding theorems: \texttt{session\_fidelity\_proc}$_\texttt{m}$ projects a typed transition to a process transition, while \texttt{session\_fidelity\_env}$_\texttt{m}$ projects it to an environment transition. The former corresponds to the forward direction of the erasure result of \citet{abs-2106-11818}, while the latter corresponds to their derivation-level session-fidelity property.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Lean4]
theorem session_fidelity_proc/-$_\texttt{m}$-/
  {/-$\der{D}$-/ : Typing n P /-$\hyp{G}$-/} {/-$\der{D}$-/' : Typing n' P' /-$\hyp{G}$-/'}
  (hStep : TypingStep/-$_\texttt{m}$-/ /-$\der{D}$-/ l /-$\der{D}$-/') :
  ProcStep/-$_\texttt{m}$-/ (proc /-$\der{D}$-/) l (proc /-$\der{D}$-/')
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Lean4]
theorem session_fidelity_env/-$_\texttt{m}$-/
  {/-$\der{D}$-/ : Typing n P /-$\hyp{G}$-/} {/-$\der{D}$-/' : Typing n' P' /-$\hyp{G}$-/'}
  (hStep : TypingStep/-$_\texttt{m}$-/ /-$\der{D}$-/ l /-$\der{D}$-/') :
  EnvStep/-$_\texttt{m}$-/ (env /-$\der{D}$-/) l (env /-$\der{D}$-/')
\end{lstlisting}

Both projection proofs proceed by induction on the typed transition derivation, and most constructors map directly to their process-level or environment-level counterpart. The main additional work occurs in the name-binding prefix cases \texttt{tensor} and \texttt{parr}. For the process projection, the free-name side conditions required by \texttt{ProcStep}$_{\texttt{m}}$ are reconstructed by selecting an auxiliary fresh name, applying the corresponding all-fresh typing lemma, and combining \path{Typing_f_eq_names}, \path{Typing_preserves_linearity}, and the fact that opening preserves existing free-name occurrences. For the environment projection, the same cases require only the all-fresh typing lemma and \path{Typing_preserves_linearity}, since the target relation records the evolution of typing resources rather than freshness of an opened process continuation. The remaining constructors reuse the structural and freshness information carried by the typed transition. The Lean statements of the resulting process- and environment-projection theorems are given in \cref{lst:app-sf-proc,lst:app-sf-env}.


\paragraph{Typability} The target lifting theorem is formulated in \path{Typability.lean}. It corresponds to the reverse direction of the erasure correspondence: given a process transition from a well-typed process, the transition should lift to the level of typing derivations. Concretely, if $\judge{n}{P}{\hyp{G}}$ and $\phl{P} \xrightarrow{l} \phl{P'}$, then there should exist a hyperenvironment $\hyp{G}'$ and a typing derivation $\judge{n}{P'}{\hyp{G}'}$ connected to the source derivation by a typed transition labelled $l$. The proof is complete for the multiplicative cases except \texttt{res}, whose remaining obligation is discussed in \cref{sec:discussion}.

In the paper presentation, erasure is expressed through a homomorphism condition, which can be read extensionally as stating that, for each label $l$, the process successors reachable from $\mathit{proc}(\der{D})$ by an $l$-labelled transition are exactly the projections of the derivation successors reachable from $\der{D}$ by the same label. In the Lean formalisation, transitions are represented as inductive relations, and this correspondence is instead formulated through projection and lifting lemmas. The theorem \path{session_fidelity_proc}$_{\texttt{m}}$ establishes the projection from typed derivation steps to process steps, while \path{typability_subject_reduction}$_{\texttt{m}}$ develops the converse lifting direction for transitions of well-typed processes. This relational formulation avoids introducing explicit sets of successors and allows the proofs to proceed by induction on the relevant transition derivations.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Lean4]
theorem typability_subject_reduction/-$_\texttt{m}$-/
  (/-$\der{D}$-/ : Typing n P /-$\hyp{G}$-/) (hPS : ProcStep/-$_\texttt{m}$-/ P l P') :
  /-$\exists$-/ (/-$\hyp{G}$-/' : HyperEnv) (/-$\der{D}$-/' : Typing n P' /-$\hyp{G}$-/'),
    TypingStep/-$_\texttt{m}~\der{D}$-/ l /-$\der{D}$-/'
\end{lstlisting}

This theorem forms the technical core of the converse lifting direction. Its proof proceeds by induction on the process transition derivation. Each case inverts the source typing derivation to recover the typing rule compatible with the observed process shape, then constructs a target hyperenvironment and typing derivation for the resulting process.

The proof has two main technical obligations. First, it must reconcile the concrete freshness witnesses used by \texttt{ProcStep}$_\texttt{m}$ with the co-finite premises of the typing rules. For example, a process transition fixes a concrete name introduced by a prefix, while the corresponding typing rule provides derivations uniformly for all names outside some finite set. Auxiliary \emph{all fresh} lemmas bridge this gap by showing that the co-finite premise can be instantiated with the concrete name appearing in the process step. Second, in the \path{one_bot}, \path{tensor_parr} and \path{res} cases, the proof must rearrange hyperenvironments to expose the components containing the communicating endpoints. In particular, transition under restriction requires opening the restricted process with fresh endpoints, applying the induction hypothesis to the opened body, and reconstructing the target hyperenvironment up to permutation. This reconstruction remains unsolved for the \path{res} case.

\paragraph{The res Case}
The remaining incomplete case is \texttt{res}. Inverting the source typing derivation exposes the body of the restriction under two fresh dual endpoints, yielding a co-finite premise of the form:
\begin{highlight}
\[
  \forall~\phl{x} \notin L, \forall~\phl{y} \notin L, \phl{x} \neq \phl{y} \to
    \judge{n}{\dopen{Q}{x,y}}{\hyp{H} \pp_h [\cht{x}{A} \texttt{::} \Gamma] \pp_h [\cht{y}{\dual{A}} \texttt{::} \Delta]}
\]
\end{highlight}

The process transition already supplies concrete fresh representatives for these bound endpoints. Using the freshness premises of the process step together with the name-correspondence invariant \path{Typing_f_eq_names}, the current proof establishes that these representatives are also fresh for the hyperenvironment components exposed by inversion. The lemma \path{Typing_res_all_fresh} can then instantiate the co-finite typing premise at the concrete names $x$ and $y$, allowing the induction hypothesis to be applied to the opened body.

The unresolved difficulty arises after this application of the induction hypothesis, as it returns a typed transition for the opened target body under an existentially determined generic target hyperenvironment. To reconstruct a typing derivation for the closed target process, this hyperenvironment must be reorganised into a form that exposes the two dual endpoint resources required by the enclosing \texttt{cut} constructor. In particular, the proof needs sufficient structural information to recover components containing $\cht{x}{A}$ and $\cht{y}{\dual{A}}$ in the post-transition hyperenvironment, or to account for the case in which the transition has merged the relevant resources into a single component.

The established name-preservation results are insufficient for this reconstruction. The lemma \path{TypingStep}$_{\texttt{m}}$\path{_names_preserved} records that names unaffected by the transition label remain present after the transition, but it does not identify the environment components in which those names occur. Membership of $x$ and $y$ in the post-transition name set therefore does not by itself imply that the target hyperenvironment can be permuted into the form:
\begin{highlight}  
  \[\thl{\hyp{H}' \pp_h [\cht{x}{A} \texttt{::} \Gamma'] \pp_h [\cht{y}{\dual{A}} \texttt{::} \Delta']}\]
\end{highlight}

The current proof attempt therefore introduces stronger auxiliary invariants for typed transitions, stated in \cref{lst:app-block-pres}. These invariants progressively strengthen component preservation from transporting an unaffected hyperenvironment component, to tracking a distinguished endpoint-bearing component, to recovering the two dual components required under restriction. The final invariant, \path{TypingStep}$_{\texttt{m}}$\path{_preserves_disjoint_or_merges}, is intended to supply the post-transition hyperenvironment structure needed in the \texttt{res} case of \path{typability_subject_reduction}$_{\texttt{m}}$ to reconstruct the enclosing \texttt{cut} derivation. The remaining work concerns the nested restriction arguments required to establish this stronger component-level preservation result. This exposes a proof-engineering consequence of representing hyperenvironments as lists modulo permutation, since reasoning about the persistence and placement of particular typed resources requires dedicated reconstruction lemmas.

\paragraph{Subject reduction} The subject-reduction statement is formulated in \path{SubjectReduction.lean}. It states that, if a well-typed process takes a process transition, then the target process is well typed under some evolved hyperenvironment, and that this hyperenvironment evolves under the same label. Once \path{typability_subject_reduction}$_\texttt{m}$ is completed, the result follows directly by applying the environment simulation \path{session_fidelity_env}$_\texttt{m}$ to the lifted typed transition.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Lean4]
theorem subject_reduction/-$_\texttt{m}$-/
  (/-$\der{D}$-/ : Typing n P /-$\hyp{G}$-/) (hPS : ProcStep/-$_\texttt{m}$-/ P l P') :
  /-$\exists$-/ /-$\hyp{G}$-/', EnvStep/-$_\texttt{m}$-/ /-$\hyp{G}$-/ l /-$\hyp{G}$-/' /-$\land$-/ (Typing n P' /-$\hyp{G}$-/') := by
  obtain /-$\langle$-//-$\hyp{G}$-/', /-$\der{D}$-/', hTS/-$\rangle$-/ := typability_subject_reduction/-$_\texttt{m}$-/ /-$\der{D}$-/ hPS
  have hES := session_fidelity_env/-$_\texttt{m}$-/ hTS
  exact /-$\langle$-//-$\hyp{G}$-/', hES, /-$\der{D}$-/'/-$\rangle$-/
\end{lstlisting}

The proof is a direct composition of the previous results: \path{typability_subject_reduction}$_\texttt{m}$ lifts the process step to a typed derivation step, and \path{session_fidelity_env}$_\texttt{m}$ projects that typed step to the corresponding environment transition. This realises the process-level session fidelity result corresponding to Corollary~4.10 of \citet{abs-2106-11818}.

\paragraph{Interpretation} The mechanised metatheory therefore establishes both projections from typed derivation transitions and completes the reverse lifting argument for all multiplicative cases except \texttt{res}. Completion of that remaining case would yield process-level session fidelity for the mechanised \pimll{} fragment by composing the lifting result with the environment projection.

The paper additionally establishes broader behavioural and concurrency results, including similarity and bisimilarity, the diamond property, serialisation, non-interference, readiness, and productivity~\citep{abs-2106-11818}. These results are not mechanised in the present development and are discussed as directions for future work in \cref{sec:future}.
