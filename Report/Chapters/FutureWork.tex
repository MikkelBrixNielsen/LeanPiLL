\section{Future Work}\label{sec:future}
The most immediate directions for future work concern completing the remaining session-fidelity proof obligation and extending the dynamic metatheory to the full \pill{} calculus.

\paragraph{Completing the multiplicative fragment}
The immediate remaining proof obligation is the \texttt{res} case of \texttt{typability\_subject\_reduction}$_\texttt{m}$. This case requires reconstructing the post-transition hyperenvironment after stepping the opened body of a restriction. The key missing ingredient is a block-preservation argument for \texttt{TypingStep}$_\texttt{m}$, showing that hyperenvironment components not involved in a transition are preserved up to permutation. This should provide the infrastructure needed to rebuild the outer \texttt{cut} typing derivation after applying the induction hypothesis to the opened body.

\paragraph{Co-finite quantification in \texttt{ProcStep}}
The current \texttt{ProcStep}$_\texttt{m}$ relation uses concrete freshness witnesses for binding constructors, while the typing rules use co-finite quantification. This asymmetry introduces proof overhead in the typability theorem, where the specific concrete names chosen by \texttt{ProcStep}$_\texttt{m}$ must be reconciled with the universally quantified premises of the corresponding typing rules. Refactoring \texttt{ProcStep}$_\texttt{m}$ to use co-finite quantification for its binding constructors would align the process and typing relations more closely and could reduce the need for auxiliary \emph{all fresh} lemmas. It may also make some explicit freshness and post-state disjointness premises derivable rather than primitive, although its effect on the erasure simulation proofs remains to be checked.

\paragraph{Extending to full \pill{}}
Once the multiplicative fragment is complete, extending the formalisation to the full \pill{} calculus appears to require additional proof engineering rather than a new formal architecture. The static model is already defined for the full calculus, and many typing inversion lemmas for the additive, polymorphic, axiom, and exponential constructs are already available.

The additive synchronisation cases have the same general hyperenvironment shape as the \texttt{tensor\_parr} case, replacing the multiplicative pair $\thl{A \otimes B}$ and $\thl{A \parr B}$ with the corresponding additive types. The unit-like additive cases mirror the structure of the already proved \texttt{one} and \texttt{bot} cases. The polymorphic interaction additionally relies on the type-substitution and shifting lemmas developed for the locally nameless representation.

The exponential and axiom-cut cases are more involved. Server use, duplication, and disposal generate larger continuation processes and manipulate dependency environments, while axiom-cut additionally involves name substitution. Nevertheless, the required ingredients appear to be the same as in the current development: typing inversion, freshness generation, substitution preservation, local closure preservation, and hyperenvironment permutation and reconstruction.

A prior revision of the formalisation included step relation constructors for the full calculus together with the auxiliary \texttt{buildDup} and \texttt{buildDisp} constructions needed for server duplication and disposal. Since well-typedness of these constructions has already been established, parts of this earlier infrastructure may be reusable when extending the current dynamic metatheory.

\paragraph{Structural congruence}
A further direction is to mechanise process-level structural congruence and prove that it preserves typing and transitions. This would internalise the commutative, associative, and unit laws of parallel composition, as well as scope manipulation for restriction, rather than requiring these rearrangements to be handled manually through exchange rules and hyperenvironment permutation lemmas. Scope extrusion is the most delicate part in the locally nameless setting, since moving a process under a restriction binder requires explicit De Bruijn index management. An alternative is to add selected symmetry and reassociation principles directly to the transition relation; whether this is simpler than a full congruence development remains open.

\paragraph{Behavioural metatheory}
The paper establishes a range of further results that are not mechanised in the current formalisation, including similarity, bisimilarity, behavioural congruence, the diamond property, serialisation, non-interference, and readiness~\citep{abs-2106-11818}. These results build on the operational semantics and would be natural extensions once the remaining session-fidelity infrastructure is completed.

\paragraph{Proof automation}
A recurring cost throughout the formalisation is the manual rearrangement of hyperenvironments using permutation, disjointness, name-set, and freshness lemmas. Building a dedicated \texttt{aesop} rule set equipped with these lemmas could automate much of this framing work and significantly reduce proof size. This would not replace the main inversion arguments, such as the remaining \texttt{res} case, but it could remove much of the repetitive bookkeeping around them. Similarly, aligning \texttt{ProcStep}$_\texttt{m}$ more closely with the co-finite style of the typing rules could reduce the number of explicit freshness-bridging obligations.
