\section{Future Work}\label{sec:future}
The most immediate directions for future work concern completing the operational semantics and extending the formalisation to cover the full \pill{} calculus.

\paragraph{Completing the multiplicative fragment}
The current session fidelity proofs cover \pimll{} with the exception of the restriction rule. Completing this case would require additional reasoning about how restriction interacts with itself under synchronisation, and how the associated hyperenvironment components are preserved and rearranged across steps. The hyperenvironment permutation infrastructure already developed for the other cases provides the necessary foundation; the remaining work is primarily in constructing the specific extraction lemmas for the restriction-under-restriction scenario.

\paragraph{Co-finite quantification in ProcStep}
The current \texttt{ProcStep}$_\texttt{m}$ relation uses existential freshness conditions for binding constructors, while the typing rules use co-finite quantification. This asymmetry introduces proof overhead in the typability theorem, where the specific concrete names chosen by \texttt{ProcStep}$_\texttt{m}$ must be reconciled with the universally quantified premises of the corresponding typing rules. Refactoring \texttt{ProcStep}$_\texttt{m}$ to use co-finite quantification for its binding constructors, specifically \texttt{tensor}, \texttt{one\_bot}, and \texttt{tensor\_parr}, would eliminate this mismatch, remove the \texttt{all\_fresh} helper infrastructure, and likely allow the post-state disjointness premise \texttt{hD2} to be dropped entirely. Whether this refactoring also resolves the remaining \texttt{sorry} cases in \texttt{typability\_subject\_reduction}$_\texttt{m}$ is the primary open question and the main motivation for pursuing this direction.

\paragraph{Extending to full \pill{}}
Once the multiplicative fragment is complete, extending the formalisation to the full \pill{} calculus would require reinstating the step relation constructors for the additive, exponential, and polymorphic fragments. A prior revision of the formalisation included these constructors together with the auxiliary \texttt{buildDup} and \texttt{buildDisp} process constructions needed for the duplication and disposal rules. The static model, processes, types, environments, and typing judgements, is already defined for the full calculus, so the remaining work is concentrated in the operational semantics and session fidelity proofs.

\paragraph{Structural congruence}
Mechanising structural congruence would address the artificial stuck states that arise from the strict binary tree structure of the process syntax. The most practically significant laws are commutativity and associativity of parallel composition, which are needed for the \rname{Syn} rule to apply to processes that are not immediate siblings, as discussed in \cref{sec:discussion}. Scope extrusion introduces additional complexity in the locally nameless setting, since pulling a process under a restriction binder requires explicit De Bruijn shifting. Rather than adding a full congruence relation to the process syntax, a more tractable approach may be to extend the transition system with explicit symmetric structural rules, such as a \texttt{syn-comm} constructor, which would allow transitions modulo the relevant congruences while preserving the syntactic stability needed for proof search in Lean.

\paragraph{Behavioural metatheory}
The paper establishes a range of further results that are not mechanised in the current formalisation, including bisimilarity and congruence (Theorems~4.5, 4.9, 4.10 in \citet{abs-2106-11818}), the diamond property (Theorem~4.15), serialisation (Fact~4.18), non-interference (Theorem~4.20), and readiness (Theorem~4.21). These results build directly on session fidelity and would become accessible once the session fidelity proofs are complete.

\paragraph{Proof automation}
A recurring cost throughout the formalisation is the manual rearrangement of hyperenvironments using permutation and disjointness lemmas. Building a dedicated \texttt{aesop} rule set equipped with the hyperenvironment permutation and disjointness lemmas could automate much of this framing work and significantly reduce proof size across the development.

\paragraph{Extending the metatheory to full \pill{}}
The current metatheory targets the multiplicative fragment, but much of the infrastructure is already formulated for the full calculus. In particular, the static typing relation and many of its inversion lemmas cover the additive, polymorphic, axiom, and exponential constructs. Extending the dynamic metatheory therefore appears to be mainly a matter of adding the corresponding \texttt{TypingStep} constructors and reusing the existing proof architecture.

The additive cases should follow the same general pattern as the solved unit-like prefix cases: invert the source typing derivation, construct the corresponding typed step, and preserve the hyperenvironment structure. The additive synchronization rules under restriction have the same overall shape as the solved multiplicative communication cases, replacing tensor/parr endpoints with selection/choice endpoints. Similarly, the polymorphic synchronization case follows the existing communication-under-restriction pattern, but additionally requires the type-substitution and shifting lemmas developed for the locally nameless representation.

The exponential and axiom-cut rules are likely to require more proof engineering. Server use, duplication, and disposal generate larger continuation processes and manipulate dependency environments, while axiom-cut performs name substitution under a cut. However, these cases do not appear to require new conceptual infrastructure beyond the existing ingredients: typing inversion, freshness generation, substitution preservation, local closure preservation, and hyperenvironment permutation/reconstruction.

\paragraph{Extending to full \pill{}}
Once the multiplicative fragment is complete, extending the formalisation to the full \pill{} calculus is more mechanical than conceptual. The static model is already defined for the full calculus, and the typing inversion lemmas --- which represent the most labour-intensive part of the session fidelity proofs --- are already in place and would be plug-and-play for the new cases.

The additive cases (\texttt{selectL\_choice} and \texttt{selectR\_choice}) have identical hyperenvironment structure to the \texttt{tensor\_parr} case, differing only in that $\thl{A \otimes B}$ and $\thl{A \parr B}$ are replaced by $\thl{A \oplus B}$ and $\thl{A \with B}$. The proofs should follow the same strategy directly. Similarly, the unit-like cases for selection (\texttt{oplus\_1}, \texttt{oplus\_2}) mirror the structure of the \texttt{one} and \texttt{bot} cases, which have already been proved.

The quantifier case (\texttt{forall\_exists}) stepping under restriction would require a hyperenvironment extraction lemma, as would the exponential interaction (\texttt{bang\_quest}), contraction (\texttt{bang\_contract}), weakening (\texttt{bang\_weaken}), and the axiom-cut interaction (\texttt{axcut}). The last of these additionally involves a name substitution step. However, once the \texttt{res} case extraction lemma is completed --- which is the primary open problem in the current development --- these cases are expected to follow analogous reasoning, as they all involve the same pattern of isolating components under restriction and reconstructing the hyperenvironment after interaction.

A prior revision of the formalisation included step relation constructors for the full calculus together with the auxiliary \texttt{buildDup} and \texttt{buildDisp} constructions needed for server duplication and disposal. The well-typedness of these constructions has already been established, so reinstating these constructors and completing the corresponding session fidelity cases represents the remaining work for a fully mechanised account of \pill{}.
